Chapter 6: Krawzer, the Realm of Dreams

You know that moment you blink and snag a thread of sleep? At the edge between lucidity and dream, our protagonist had played so much that he finally drifted off…

Without quite understanding, half-confused, he woke with his face pressed to damp earth—the smell of leaves and moss filling his lungs. Disoriented, he tried to get up, but strong hands held his arms. He looked around, heart pounding. He was surrounded by strange figures dressed in animal hides, adorned with feathers and bones. Their faces were painted in vibrant colors; their eyes watched him with a mix of curiosity and distrust.

He tried to speak, but the sounds that left his mouth were incomprehensible—a tangle of guttural syllables. The strangers traded confused looks. One of them—stern-faced, a scar across his cheek—gave an order in an unknown tongue. Two of them lifted him and tied him with ropes made from plant fibers. He tried to break free, uselessly. He was completely at their mercy.

They led him along a narrow trail winding through dense forest. The trees were tall and imposing—thick trunks and leaves that seemed to glow with their own light. The air was humid, full of the sounds of insects and unfamiliar animals. After a long walk, they reached a clearing where wooden-and-thatch huts circled a bonfire. Children ran and played, women prepared food in clay pots, men sharpened spears and knives.

The boy was brought before the village leader, a robust elder with white hair and a piercing gaze waiting for them. He spoke in a language Gael did not understand, but his tone was firm and authoritative. In this world social position and occupation were defined by one’s elemental type. Since he was unknown, they suspected he might be a spy, and so put him to a test to learn what kind of foreigner he was. Two warriors brought four stones—each a different color: red, blue, green, and yellow. The elder pointed to each stone in turn, and the boy understood he should approach them. He touched the red stone and felt an intense heat emanate from it. Then he touched the blue stone and felt a chill that froze his fingers. The green stone transmitted a sense of peace and calm, while the yellow filled him with energy and vitality. But something was off—their faces didn’t look pleased. The elder watched closely, but none of the stones seemed to awaken a significant response. A worried expression creased his face.

“Bring the Crystal of Revelation,” he ordered one of the warriors.

A youth approached, carefully carrying a translucent crystal in both hands. It emitted a soft multicolored light, pulsing like a heart.

The elder positioned the crystal before the boy, eyes fixed on his expression.

“Touch the crystal, young one,” he instructed. “It will reveal the true nature of your magic.”

Still confused and afraid, the boy reached out and touched the smooth surface. The instant his fingers met it, the crystal exploded into brilliant golden light, flooding the clearing with a celestial glow.

The villagers recoiled, shielding their eyes. A reverent silence fell, broken only by the boy’s ragged breathing.

The elder fell to his knees—and the others followed. They bowed before the boy, their voices rising into a chorus of pleas and apologies.

“Forgive us, great dreamer,” said the elder, voice thick with emotion. “We did not know we stood before a bearer of divine magic.”

The warriors who had captured him approached and—with trembling hands—untied his ropes. The boy stood, dizzy with confusion.

He still didn’t understand the Krawzer tongue. The sounds they made were strange and guttural—a mix of grunts, whistles, and clicks. Yet somehow he understood the meaning behind them. It was as if communication happened on a deeper level—through images, emotions, and intuition. “Krawzer,” he thought—and the word echoed in his mind like a mantra, a sound containing the essence of that strange, wondrous world. It wasn’t exactly a word, but a vibration—an energy permeating everything around him.

Gael had a fleeting memory of what he’d been doing before arriving there—he was a priest in a game with his friends…

The villagers prostrated themselves in reverence before him. He felt energy pulse inside him like a source of light and power. He raised his hands, instinctively, and golden light emanated from his fingers, wrapping the villagers in a warm, comforting glow. Fear in their eyes turned to admiration and gratitude.

The elder rose and approached, eyes shining with tears of joy.

“You are the traveler from the legends of old!” he said, voice hoarse with emotion. “You are the bearer of light—the one who will bring balance and harmony to Krawzer.”

The boy didn’t fully understand his words—but he felt the truth inside them. He had been called to a larger purpose—to a mission that transcended his understanding.

He looked at the villagers, the forest’s creatures, the starry sky overhead. He felt a deep connection to that world—to its permeating magic and the beings who lived there. But something spun out of his control. The energy would not stop flowing from his body, as if drained by an invisible force. He grew weak, dizzy, exhausted. He tried to halt the flow—but could not. It was as if his healing power had activated at a level he couldn’t contain. Images and sensations flooded his mind: sick and wounded villagers, starving and frail animals, wilted trees and plants. He realized that, despite its beauty and magic, Krawzer was also plagued by war, disease, and imbalance. Technology didn’t exist there, but magic—used irresponsibly and selfishly—had harmed the environment and living beings. Greed, hatred, and violence had left deep scars in Krawzer’s soul.

He felt that reality’s weight on his shoulders—the responsibility of healing an unknown world. The task was greater than he could bear. The energy drained rapidly; he collapsed to his knees, exhausted and powerless. Seeing his condition, they carried him into a hut and laid him on a bed of soft leaves. He fell into a deep sleep—and the healing magic continued to flow from him, spreading through the village and the surrounding forest. The sick recovered; wounded animals mended; wilted plants returned to life. The forest burst into green; wild animals returned—drawn by the vital energy emanating from the village. Krawzer was healing through the energy the boy channeled.

After three days—once the village was fully healed—he finally began to adapt; Gael was learning to contain that power. He sat up on the leaf-bed, observing the simple hut that sheltered him. Sunlight seeped through gaps in the wooden wall, gilding the interior. The door opened and the elder entered with a warm smile. His eyes were green; his skin, bronze from the sun. He wore simple coarse clothes and carried a hoe over his shoulder.

“Welcome back to the land of the living, young dreamer,” the man said, voice deep and kind. “I am Geo Lumis, the leader of this village.”

The boy tried to answer, but no words came. He still couldn’t speak the Krawzer language—but somehow he understood what Geo said.

“Don’t worry,” Geo said, noting his struggle. “My children will help you learn our tongue.”

He called two youths standing outside. Twins—a boy and a girl—with brown hair and green eyes like their father.

“Tio and Lia,” Geo introduced them. “They’ll be your guides and teachers while you’re with us.”

The twins smiled, and he felt an immediate connection. They seemed gentle and patient; he knew he’d be in good hands.

In the days that followed, Tio and Lia devoted themselves to teaching him Krawzer. They used gestures, drawings in the dirt, and plenty of patience to help him understand that language’s strange guttural sounds. He learned quickly—an agile mind soaking up the new information like a sponge. Krawzer was more than words; it was a way of connecting with nature, with magic, and with other living beings.

He learned to say “Krawzer,” and the word echoed in his mind like a mantra—a sound that seemed to hold the essence of that strange and marvelous world. He learned to say “thank you,” “please,” and “friend”—simple words expressing the gratitude, respect, and connection he felt with the Lumis and that place.

Days in the Leaf Village, as it was known, turned into weeks, and the boy—whom Tio and Lia called “Lumi”—adapted to his new life.

“Krawzer,” he repeated, pronouncing it carefully, feeling the vibration in his throat.

“Very good!” Lia smiled, green eyes shining. “Now say: ‘Thank you, Tio, for teaching me.’”

“Th… thank you, Tio,” he stammered, still grappling with the guttural sounds.

Tio—a sturdy boy with unruly hair—patted his back.

"You're learning fast, Lumi," he said. "Soon you'll speak like a true Terran."

“But…” he hesitated—still unsure of his pronunciation. “My name isn’t Lumi.”

The twins traded puzzled looks.

“What do you mean?” Lia asked. “We thought…”

“We called you Lumi because you shone with the Crystal of Revelation’s light,” Tio explained. “We thought it was your name.”

Tio told him about their ancestor Lumi—a light-mage who brought prosperity and vanished mysteriously—and how they thought the boy might be him—or his descendant.

“Oh,” he said, smiling at the confusion. “My name is Gael.”

“Gael,” Lia repeated, testing the name on her tongue. “It’s a beautiful name.”

“Welcome to the Leaf Village, Gael,” Tio said, smiling.

Gael spent his days helping with village tasks. He learned to plant seeds, harvest fruit, care for animals—and even to use earth-magic—the local specialty—to enrich the soil and speed plant growth.

“Feel the earth’s energy flowing through you,” Geo Lumis instructed, demonstrating how to guide a tree’s roots to form a natural shelter. “The earth is generous, but it demands respect.”

Gael closed his eyes, focusing on the sensation of soil under his feet and the vital energy pulsing in every leaf and grain of sand. He extended his hands and felt earth-magic respond to his touch, molding to his will.

At night, gathered around the fire, the villagers shared Krawzer’s stories and legends. Gael learned about the elemental realms—about the wars and conflicts that shaped that world—about the delicate balance between light and darkness, order and chaos. He remembered the Bees’ Secret—the looming environmental apocalypse in his own world. He wondered whether Krawzer faced similar challenges—whether magic, despite its power, could also be a source of destruction.

As stars sparked overhead and the bonfire crackled, Geo Lumis approached Gael with a thoughtful look.

“Gael,” he began—voice deep and calm—“you were fortunate we found you.”

Gael looked at him, curious.

“Fortunate?”

“Yes,” Geo nodded. “Not everyone in Krawzer would receive a bearer of light so openly. In some villages, your presence would be met with fear and distrust.”

“Why?” Gael frowned. “Isn’t light-magic good?”

Geo sighed and sat beside him, watching the dancing flames.

“Light-magic is rare and powerful, Gael. And like all forms of power, it can be used for good or evil.”

“But I don’t want to hurt anyone,” Gael protested.

“I know,” Geo said, laying a reassuring hand on his shoulder. “But not everyone is like you. In this world, every light-user also holds aptitude for shadow-magic. It’s a matter of balance—of choice.”

“Shadow-magic?” Gael shivered, remembering stories he’d heard of the Kingdom of Thunder and its relentless warriors.

“Yes,” Geo confirmed. “There is a city in Krawzer called Lullaby. It attracts light- and shadow-mages. There, they learn to control their powers—and choose their path.”

“What happens to those who choose shadow?”

“They become shadows of themselves—consumed by power and ambition,” Geo said sadly. “They turn against their own—sowing chaos and destruction wherever they go.”

Gael fell silent, absorbing Geo’s words.

“I’ll understand if you don’t want to stay, Gael,” Geo continued. “Near beings of other elements, your light tends to dissipate into spontaneous healings—as happened when you arrived. I know it can be painful to remain among us.”

“But…” Gael looked at the friendly faces around the fire. “I like it here. You welcomed me—taught me—and made me feel part of something.”

“We are grateful for your presence,” Geo smiled. “Your healing has worked wonders. Our crops are more abundant, our animals healthier, our people happier.”

“If you stay, Gael, we promise to treat you as one of our own,” Geo said, extending a hand. “You will always be welcome in the Leaf Village.”

Gael looked at the offered hand and then at the villagers’ smiling faces. Warmth spread through his chest—a sense of belonging he had never known.

“I’ll stay,” he said, gripping Geo’s hand firmly. “I’ll stay and help however I can.”
