Chapter 1: Ironsight, the City of Steel

Ironsight rose like a monument to human ambition—and to its capacity for devastation. Steel-and-glass towers pierced the gray sky. When the sun managed to slip through the pollution, it cast a sickly light that revealed the grime beneath the city’s polished veneer. Drones hummed, giant screens preached consumption, and people hurried past with hollow eyes.

In the midst of this, Gael tried to find his place. Sensitive, observant, quietly stubborn, he felt out of tune with Ironsight’s pace. He longed for trees, soil, silence—the kind of silence that held birdsong.

One afternoon he stumbled upon a flyer: Urban Gardeners Club. Community plots among concrete. The words felt like water. He went home carrying a fragile hope, only to meet the cold front of his grandparents’ expectations. Money. Status. Security. Everything that couldn’t be planted, watered, or held.

Still, he returned to the square. There, under a weary willow, an old woman tended a tiny, improvised garden. Bees skimmed the flowers with a desperate precision. And then, like a chord struck inside his mind, he heard—not with ears, but with something older—images, scents, urgency: empty hives, barren fields, a future without the hum that keeps the world alive.

Help us… time is running out… you are our keeper… awaken.

For a heartbeat, the bees drew a perfect circle in the air, and he understood that a secret had chosen him—as small and vast as the world itself.
