Chapter 2: The Bees’ Secret

The message didn’t arrive as words but as a pressure in the chest—a knowing that all things were bound together. Gael felt it while watching the last bees drift between a few stubborn flowers in the square. Images pressed into him: empty hives, silent orchards, a future without fruit.

He hunted for answers: dust-smelling books, stark documentaries, papers full of terms he learned one by one. Pesticides that confused navigation. Habitat erased by concrete. Heat bending the timing of blooms. Parasites gnawing entire colonies apart. And something else that science hadn’t named yet: a pattern in the air, geometry in the combs, as if the bees were writing to anyone who could read.

When a single bee rested on his outstretched hand, meaning flooded in. Not words—maps. Despair and stubbornness, hunger and flight-paths, the thread that connected hive to meadow, and meadow to table, and table to every family. The swarm’s intelligence pushed one truth to the surface: We are running out of time.

The city didn’t want to hear it. His grandparents dismissed it; his classmates laughed. Truth was a dangerous thing in Ironsight. So he kept it where it would stay alive: in a notebook, in a plan to start small, in the part of him that refused to harden.
